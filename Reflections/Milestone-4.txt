For spam protection I chose a honeypot and a rate limit instead of a CAPTCHA, so real visitors never have to solve a puzzle: the form has a hidden "website" field that only bots fill in, messages sent in under three seconds are dropped, and a trigger in Supabase allows at most three messages per email or visitor every ten minutes. Putting the rate limit in the database means it still works even if someone skips my form and calls Supabase directly, and RLS means visitors can only add a message, while only my account can read, mark as read or delete them. The worst layout bug I fixed was on phones: the Home page scrolled sideways because the long line in my developer.ts code card forced the whole hero column wider than the screen. I fixed it by limiting the grid column with grid-cols-1, letting the role line wrap and shrinking the code text on small screens. I also measured the contrast of every piece of text and brightened my dim grey and small red text so everything passes WCAG AA.
